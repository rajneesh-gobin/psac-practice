                                          Preface
The Grade 7 English Textbook is based on the National Curriculum Framework (2017) and
adheres to the English Teaching and Learning Syllabus (2017). In line with the Nine Years
Continuous Education policy, it has been conceived as a progression from the Grade 6 textbook
to allow a smooth transition from primary to secondary schooling. Consequently, the different
components as well as with the activity-based and interactive approaches are familiar to the
students.


The textbook constitutes the following components:


Communications skills
  Listening activities on a variety of topics aim at making students develop the ability to listen to
  an array of speakers with understanding. Speaking activities help students gradually develop
  ease, fluency and confidence while expressing themselves in English. Open-ended discussion
  or sharing activities, and structured activities targeting the acquisition of functional language
  are found in each unit. Furthermore, each unit ends with a short fun- filled activity aimed at
  improving pronunciation, diction and clarity of speech.


Reading comprehension
  Reading texts of different genres have been included to consolidate and extend students’
  ability to read with understanding and to encourage them to respond critically and creatively
  to texts. The varied themes, lay-out and content of the texts provide a rich context for the
  development of effective reading strategies that will make the learners become independent
  readers. To that end, pre-reading, while reading and post reading activities have been
  provided.


Reading for pleasure and appreciation
  The ability to derive pleasure and demonstrate appreciation of a text is an important skill
  and its contribution to the overall development of reading cannot be undermined. Each unit
  therefore includes one text that students will read for pleasure and appreciation. A glossary
  has been included to promote reader autonomy and post reading activities to provide a
  platform for sharing and discussion.


Writing
 Both creative and functional skills are developed through the writing activities that are found
 in each unit. As the students are engaged in the production of different types of texts that will
 develop their awareness that text function, lay-out and register vary according to purpose,
 reader and situation, they will develop the ability to vary the style and language in line with
 the topic and audience.
  II
Vocabulary
  Vocabulary development is an inescapable part of language learning and each unit provides
  an array of vocabulary to enrich the learners’ repertoire. The thematic approach of the
  textbook allows for the development of vocabulary across the unit rather than limiting it to
  specific activities. Oral and written activities permit the students to use words learnt, thereby
  reinforcing their knowledge.


Grammar
  Grammar is also a significant aspect of language learning and a knowledge of grammar
  rules is essential for effective communication. The learners must view grammar as a means
  to express themselves correctly and with precision, rather than an end in itself. Thus, while
  there is some attention drawn to the rules at secondary level, usage has to be promoted
  rather than rote learning.


In addition to instructions for all the activities, Teaching Tips have been included where
deemed necessary to provide guidelines or suggestions for implementation. Nevertheless, the
teaching approach will have to be adapted in line with the level and needs of the learners. It
is understood that all students do not learn at the same pace and that the use of additional
examples/samples/resources are always beneficial to the learning experience. A CD is provided
for the listening activities to help students develop familiarity with a variety of speakers and
accents. Nevertheless, Educators could read the texts aloud if deemed necessary. To that end,
all transcripts have been included in the Appendix.


It is hoped that both Educators and students enjoy working with the textbook and find the
process enriching.

Esha’s first concert

Esha’s brother, Yaj, and their cousins were talking excitedly. The New Rock Band would be giving a

“I’d like to go to the concert,” Esha replied. “But I’m sure that Mum and Dad won’t allow me.”

 The Present Perfect Tense is formed by combining the Present Simple Tense of the verb ‘to

 Has/ Have + past participle

 We use the Present Perfect Tense:
 • For incomplete actions that started in the past and continue in the present.

 • To talk about something that has happened over a period of time.
   E.g. “The Lion King” has remained a very popular film over the years.

 NOTE: We often use the words for, since, just, already and yet with the Present Perfect Tense.

The Life of Anne Frank

                                       Anne Frank is best known for having written “The Diary of a
                                       Young Girl”, in which she writes about the two years that she
                                       and her family spent in hiding during World War II. She was
                                       born on 12 June 1929 in Germany, and died in March 1945
                                       at the young age of 15. Anne was known as being a friendly
                                       girl, brimming with life, whilst her sister, Margot was the
                                       introvert of the family. Anne was Jewish.


                                       In 1933, Adolf Hitler, the leader of the political party the
                                       Nazi, took over Germany. Hitler was a very racist leader and
he did not like the Jews, whom he felt were responsible for Germany’s problems. In 1934, the Frank
family had to flee to Netherlands to avoid being sent to concentration camps, where Jews were
tortured in many horrific ways.


The family lived in the Netherlands for many years until World War II started. Hitler invaded the
Netherlands on 10 May 1940. Jews, at that time, did not have the right to work, to have businesses, to
go to the cinema, or to visit parks. In fact, most of them were rounded up and sent to concentration
camps where they had to live in deplorable conditions. The Franks, unfortunately, did not have
time to run away and were forced to remain in hiding, in a place which is now called the Secret
Annex.


During that period, Anne, who had received a red diary from her dad on her birthday, started
chronicling her days in their hiding place. This diary, which was found by her father after her
death, was published in 1947 and translated into 65 languages. Anne had named her diary ‘Kitty’
and, in it, she wrote about a number of things. At the time she wrote the diary, she did not know
that anyone would be reading her story. She wrote about how she felt, the books she read, and the
people she met.


Anne’s diary opens a window on the life of people who are forced to live in hiding for years. It
portrays the life of the Jews during World War II. The Franks had to live in a cramped place so
that they could be together as a family. They had to be very quiet to avoid being caught. They could
hardly talk and had to whisper. They could not even flush the toilet or use water properly to avoid
making any noise. Moreover, the Franks had to share the Secret Annex with other people who
needed a place to hide too.

After having lived in the Secret Annex for two years and as World War II was nearing its end, the
Franks were captured by the Nazis. They were separated and sent to concentration camps where
Anne and Margot died from Typhus disease. Only Anne’s father survived at the end of World War
II and he was the one who got her diary published when he found it.

         ACTIVITY 7: Adverbs of Frequency and Degree

                This is the story of two friends, Jack and Ram.

                                       Jack always goes to his weekly sports meetings. Ram
                                       never listens to Jack and he rarely accompanies him to
                                       the gym. Ram hardly takes care of his health while Jack
                                       is extremely careful. Jack was quite surprised when Ram
                                       started participating fully in the gym sessions.


                                       The words in bold are adverbs. The story has two types of
adverbs: adverbs of frequency and adverbs of degree. Can you guess their use?


ADVERBS OF FREQUENCY
Adverbs of frequency answer the question ‘How frequently?’ or ‘How often?’
They indicate how often something happens, e.g. weekly, never, rarely.



ADVERBS OF DEGREE
Adverbs of degree answer the question ‘To what extent?’
They indicate the strength or intensity of something that happens, e.g. hardly, extremely, quite, fully.

1a. Listening - Listen to the text carefully.

                                             Egypt
Egypt is a fascinating country that attracts many people. While many enjoy visiting its famous
places, most people are keen to discover its rich history.

Ancient Egypt is a topic of great interest. Life in Ancient Egypt revolved around the river Nile
and the fertile land along its banks. Farmers in Egypt were completely dependent on the river.
They developed irrigation methods to control the flow of the water so that crops could grow
during both rainy and dry seasons. During the flooding season, from June to September, much
of the flood water was stored in tanks and ponds. Ditches were dug to carry this water to the
fields when there was very little rainfall.

Though most of the people in Ancient Egypt were farmers, the land was owned by the Pharaoh,
the temples or by rich noble families. The Pharaoh was the most important person in Ancient
Egypt. He was the political and religious ruler and was even considered as a god. Most Egyptians
prayed and worked for his personal welfare. Peasants paid labour tax, and at times worked on
public projects such as irrigation or construction works to show their loyalty to the Pharaoh.

The ancient Egyptians worshipped many gods and goddesses, such as Ra, the sun god; Isis, the
goddess of nature; Horus, the god of war; and Osiris, the god of the dead. Generally, the gods
were worshipped by the priests in temples. However, on special occasions, they were brought
out and shown to the public. The common men worshipped small statues in their homes and
wore charms and amulets to protect themselves from evil.

Ancient Egypt is widely known for its achievements in the development of writing, that is
hieroglyphics; in mathematics and architecture; irrigation and agricultural methods; as well as
in ship building. Some of the world’s most famous monuments are also found in Egypt: the Giza
pyramids, the Great Sphinx, the ancient temples of Luxor, the Mosque of Ibn Tulun, and the
Egyptian Museum. These never cease to fascinate visitors.

Although most tourists travel to Egypt mainly to visit places related to the great Egyptian
civilisation, some of them also enjoy sports like snorkeling and diving along the Red Sea coast.
Camel trips into the mountains of Sinai or tours to remote oases of the desert are also very
appealing to travellers. Cruising the Nile is yet another popular way of visiting upper Egypt.
It is not surprising, therefore, that Egypt is known as a country of wisdom, mystery and discovery.
