SECTION A — READING COMPREHENSION   [30 marks]

Read the passage carefully, then answer ALL the questions that follow.

The Digital Generation: How Social Media Is Reshaping Young Lives

The rise of social media has transformed the way teenagers communicate, learn, and perceive themselves. Platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, and Snapchat have become integral parts of adolescent life, with the average teenager spending over three hours daily scrolling through feeds, posting updates, and engaging with content. While these platforms offer remarkable opportunities for connection and creativity, researchers and parents alike are raising serious concerns about their long-term effects on young people's mental health and social development.

One of the most frequently cited benefits of social media is the ability to maintain friendships across distances. For teenagers who have moved to new neighbourhoods or schools, digital platforms provide a vital lifeline to existing friendships. Moreover, social media can serve as a powerful tool for self-expression, allowing young people to share their talents — whether through art, music, or writing — with a global audience that would have been unimaginable a generation ago.

However, the darker side of social media cannot be ignored. Studies conducted by public health organisations have found that certain platforms are strongly linked to increased rates of anxiety, depression, and body image issues among young users. The constant stream of carefully curated images creates unrealistic standards of beauty and success that many teenagers feel compelled to measure themselves against. When they inevitably fall short, feelings of inadequacy and low self-worth follow.

Cyberbullying represents another grave concern. Unlike traditional bullying, which is confined to the school environment, online harassment can follow victims into their homes and into the early hours of the morning. The anonymity that the internet provides can embolden bullies, making their attacks more vicious and harder to trace. Victims often suffer in silence, afraid that reporting the abuse will worsen their situation or that adults will not fully understand the online world.

Furthermore, the addictive nature of social media platforms is not accidental. These applications are engineered by teams of designers and psychologists to maximise user engagement, using techniques such as variable reward schedules — the same mechanism found in gambling — to keep users scrolling for just a little longer. The result is a generation that struggles to focus, to tolerate boredom, and to simply exist without a screen nearby.

The challenge for parents, educators, and policymakers is not to eliminate social media from young people's lives — a task that is both impractical and counterproductive — but to equip them with the critical thinking skills and digital literacy needed to navigate this complex landscape safely and confidently.
