SOURCE B
The Export Processing Zone (EPZ) was established in Mauritius in 1971 as a strategy to attract foreign investment and create employment by offering tax incentives, duty-free importation of raw materials, and streamlined regulations for export-oriented manufacturers. Primarily focused on the textile and garment industry, the EPZ transformed Mauritius from a monoculture sugar economy into a manufacturing nation within a decade. Women made up over 70% of the EPZ workforce, marking a significant shift in gender roles and women's economic participation. At its peak in the late 1980s and 1990s, the EPZ employed over 90,000 workers and generated more than 50% of Mauritius's export earnings. However, the removal of preferential trade agreements and competition from lower-cost Asian manufacturers led to a sharp decline from the early 2000s. Today, Mauritius has shifted focus towards higher value-added sectors such as ICT, financial services, and biotechnology.
Adapted from: Bank of Mauritius / Ministry of Finance, Economic History of Mauritius

SOURCE C
"Despite impressive economic progress since independence, Mauritius continues to face significant social challenges. Income inequality remains a concern, with the richest 20% of households earning approximately seven times more than the poorest 20%. Poverty is concentrated among specific groups including elderly persons living alone, single-parent families, and residents of certain peri-urban communities. Access to quality education is not yet equal: children from disadvantaged backgrounds are more likely to underperform in examinations and less likely to pursue tertiary education. Gender inequality in the labour market persists, with women earning on average 16% less than men for equivalent work. However, government social programmes — including free education, free healthcare, social housing, and income support — have kept the absolute poverty rate below 1%. The challenge is now to build a more equitable society where all citizens share in the country's prosperity."
Adapted from: National Human Development Report, Mauritius, 2024
