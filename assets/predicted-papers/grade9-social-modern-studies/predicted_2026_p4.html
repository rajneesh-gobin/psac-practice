SECTION B — Source-Based Questions [30 marks] Answer ALL questions in this section.
SOURCE A
The African Union (AU) was established in 2002 to replace the Organisation of African Unity (OAU), which was founded in 1963. The OAU focused primarily on achieving independence for African colonies and opposing apartheid in South Africa. Once these goals were largely achieved, African leaders recognised the need for a new organisation that could address the continent's development challenges, promote good governance, and prevent conflicts. The AU's founding document, the Constitutive Act, commits member states to the promotion of democratic principles, human rights, the rule of law, and sustainable development. Key AU institutions include the Assembly of Heads of State, the Commission (its executive body), the Peace and Security Council, and the Pan-African Parliament. The AU's Agenda 2063 vision describes Africa as "The Africa We Want" — a prosperous, integrated, peaceful, and democratic continent driven by its own citizens and able to take its rightful place in the global community.
Adapted from: African Union Commission, AU Overview Document, 2023

SOURCE C
"The world today faces a set of interconnected challenges that no single nation can solve alone. Climate change threatens the stability of ecosystems and livelihoods globally, with small island developing states facing existential risks. The COVID-19 pandemic exposed deep inequalities in access to healthcare, vaccines, and economic support between wealthy and developing nations. Conflicts in various regions continue to displace millions of people, creating the largest refugee crisis since World War II. Terrorism, cyber-attacks, and the spread of disinformation threaten the security of democratic institutions. Meanwhile, rising nationalism and geopolitical competition between major powers such as the United States and China have weakened multilateral institutions and made international cooperation more difficult. The lesson of the 21st century is clear: global problems require global solutions, and institutions like the United Nations must be strengthened, reformed, and made more representative to meet these challenges effectively."
Adapted from: UN Secretary-General's Report on Global Challenges, 2024
