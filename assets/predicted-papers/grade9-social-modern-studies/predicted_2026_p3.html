SECTION B — Source-Based Questions [30 marks] Answer ALL questions in this section.
SOURCE A
As a small island developing state (SIDS) located in the Indian Ocean, Mauritius is highly vulnerable to the effects of climate change. Sea-level rise of 3–5mm per year threatens low-lying coastal areas and the country's world-famous beaches and lagoons. Increased intensity of tropical cyclones poses risks to infrastructure, agriculture, and tourism. Ocean warming and acidification have led to coral bleaching events, destroying reef ecosystems that support fish populations and protect coastlines from wave erosion. Mauritius is also grappling with plastic pollution in its marine environment, deforestation of indigenous forests for agriculture and development, and the spread of invasive plant and animal species that threaten its unique flora and fauna. Despite contributing less than 0.01% of global greenhouse gas emissions, Mauritius faces some of the most severe impacts of climate change. The government has committed to achieving 60% renewable energy by 2030 as part of its climate strategy.
Adapted from: Ministry of Environment, Mauritius National Climate Change Adaptation Strategy, 2024

"Article 1: All human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights. They are endowed with reason and conscience and should act towards one another in a spirit of brotherhood.
