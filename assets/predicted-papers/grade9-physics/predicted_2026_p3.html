Work, energy and power

(a)
Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained by the student.[2]

(b)
Calculate the useful power output of the student.[2]

(c)
State one energy transformation that occurs in the student's muscles as they climb.[1]

SECTION C – Long Structured Questions  [15 marks]    Answer BOTH questions.
20.
Energy conservation and power

A trolley of mass 5 kg is released from rest at the top of a ramp 3 m high. The trolley rolls to the bottom and reaches a speed of 7 m/s.
Fig. 20.1 – Trolley on a ramp
(a)
Calculate the gravitational potential energy of the trolley at the top of the ramp.[2]
GPE = J
(b)
Calculate the kinetic energy of the trolley at the bottom of the ramp.[2]
KE = J
(c)
The GPE and KE values are not equal. Explain why, and state where the missing energy has gone.[2]
(d)
Calculate the efficiency of the ramp as an energy conversion device.[2]
Efficiency = %
(e)
If the trolley travels the length of the slope (5 m) in 2 seconds at the end, estimate the average power delivered by gravity along the slope. Use W = F×d and P = W/t, taking the component of weight along the slope as 30 N.[1]
Power = W

Electromagnetic induction and a.c. generation

A bar magnet is pushed into a coil of 200 turns. A student uses a galvanometer to detect the induced current.
Fig. 21.1 – Bar magnet entering a coil
(a)
State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction in your own words.[2]
(b)
The student pushes the magnet in, holds it still, then pulls it out. Describe what the galvanometer reading does in each stage and explain why.[3]
(c)
State two ways the student could increase the size of the induced e.m.f.[1]
