SECTION B — Structured Questions
B1 — Chromosomes and DNA
PartAccepted AnswerMarks
(a) Chromosomes are found in the nucleus of cells and are made of DNA (1). Genes are specific sections/segments of DNA that code for a particular characteristic or protein (1). 2
(b) During fertilisation, a gamete from each parent fuses together (1). If each gamete had 46 chromosomes, the resulting zygote would have 92, which would be fatal/incompatible with life; having 23 ensures the zygote receives the correct number of 46 chromosomes (1). 2
(c) A mutation is a random/spontaneous change in the DNA/genetic material of an organism (1). Example: sickle cell anaemia / Down syndrome / albinism / any other valid mutation (1) — only 1 mark total for this part. 1

B4 — The Excretory System
PartAccepted AnswerMarks
(a) Any THREE correct pairs (1 mark each): Urea → Kidneys / Carbon dioxide → Lungs / Water → Kidneys, Lungs, or Skin / Bile pigments → Liver (excreted via gut) / Excess salts → Kidneys or Skin. 3

C2 — Respiratory and Excretory Systems [7 marks]
PartAccepted AnswerMarks
(a) Nose (nasal cavity) → Pharynx / throat → Larynx / voice box → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli. Must include at least 4 named structures in correct order for 2 marks; 1 mark for 2–3 correct structures in order. 2
(b) During filtration: both glucose and urea (along with water and salts) are filtered out of the blood into the Bowman’s capsule / filtrate (1). During reabsorption: glucose is completely reabsorbed back into the blood by active transport in the tubules; urea is NOT reabsorbed — it remains in the filtrate and is excreted as urine (1). 2
(c) Cilia normally sweep mucus (and trapped dust/bacteria) upwards towards the throat to be swallowed or expelled (1). When cilia are damaged, mucus and pathogens/bacteria accumulate in the airways, increasing the risk of infection / bronchitis / smoker’s cough (1). 2
