Question 4A — Reading Comprehension: Informational Text (10 marks)

Read the text below, then complete the sentences that follow.

The Great Village Clean-Up of Mahébourg

The small town of Mahébourg, on the south-east coast of Mauritius, is well known for its community spirit. Every year, residents come together for the Great Village Clean-Up — a weekend event that has been running for over fifteen years.

The clean-up takes place every January, when volunteers of all ages gather early in the morning at the village square. Organisers give out gloves, bags and tools, and the volunteers split into teams to cover different areas. Some teams collect litter from streets and public parks. Others paint old benches, repair paths or plant flowers along the roadsides.

Young people play an important role in the event. Secondary school students collect litter from the beach and go door to door collecting newspapers, glass bottles and plastic containers for recycling. The Scouts and Girl Guides set up refreshment stalls and give out water and snacks to the other volunteers.

The community garden is another central feature of the project. Volunteers work together to plant vegetables, herbs and fruit trees that any resident of the town can harvest freely. This benefits families who cannot afford to buy fresh produce from the market.

By the end of the weekend, the town looks completely transformed. The streets are clean, the parks are tidy and the garden is full of young plants. Residents celebrate with a shared meal prepared by local women’s groups. Everyone goes home feeling proud of what they have built together as a community.

Question 4B — Reading Comprehension: Narrative (15 marks)

The New Girl

Maya had been at Rivière du Rempart Primary School for three days, but she had not yet spoken to anyone. She sat alone at lunch each day, eating her rice and watching the other children play loudly in the yard. She missed her old school, her old friends and the home she had left behind.

On the fourth morning, a cheerful girl named Sadie sat down beside her in class. Without saying a word, Sadie placed a small packet of biscuits on Maya’s desk.

“I made too many,” Sadie said simply. “You can have some.”

Maya looked up, surprised. “Thank you,” she whispered.

During lunch, Sadie invited Maya to sit with her group of friends. At first, Maya answered only in short sentences and kept her eyes on her plate. But Sadie’s friends were friendly and funny, and before long Maya found herself laughing along with them.

That afternoon, the class teacher, Mrs Ghoorah, noticed that Maya’s face had changed. She no longer looked pale and worried. She was smiling.

“How are you settling in?” Mrs Ghoorah asked her after school.

“Better,” said Maya. “Much better.”

On her way home, Maya thought about Sadie’s small act of kindness — the biscuits and the invitation to join the group. Such a small thing, yet it had changed everything. She decided that she too would always look out for anyone who seemed lonely or new at school.

That evening, she told her mother she liked her new school. And she meant it.
