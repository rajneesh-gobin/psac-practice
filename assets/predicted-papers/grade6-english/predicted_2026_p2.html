Question 4A — Reading Comprehension: Informational Text (10 marks)

Read the text below, then complete the sentences that follow.

The Aapravasi Ghat

The Aapravasi Ghat is a small but very significant historical site located on the waterfront of Port Louis, the capital city of Mauritius. It was built by the British government in 1849 to receive workers who came from India. These workers were called “indentured labourers” — men and women who had signed contracts to work on the sugar cane plantations of Mauritius in exchange for a small wage and accommodation.

Between 1834 and 1924, more than half a million Indian workers arrived through the Aapravasi Ghat. They came from different parts of India, including Bihar, Madras and Bengal. The sea journey to Mauritius was long and difficult, often lasting several weeks. Upon arrival, workers were examined by doctors and registered before being sent to plantations across the island.

Life for the indentured labourers was very hard. They worked long hours in the sugar cane fields under the hot sun. They lived in simple barracks near the plantations. Many workers fell ill and missed their families deeply. However, over generations, they and their descendants became an important and valued part of Mauritian society, enriching the culture, food, music and traditions of the island.

In 2006, the Aapravasi Ghat was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognising its importance as a site of human migration and labour history. Today it is open to visitors and serves as a powerful reminder of the courage and sacrifice of the indentured labourers — a symbol of Mauritius’s rich and diverse heritage.

Question 4B — Reading Comprehension: Narrative (15 marks)

Anita and the Old Chest

Anita had always been a curious girl who loved history. One afternoon, while helping her grandmother clear out the attic, she discovered an old wooden chest hidden behind a dusty wardrobe. It was locked, but Grandmother produced a small brass key from her jewellery box.

Inside the chest, they found faded photographs, a bundle of handwritten letters tied with string, and a folded piece of cloth that turned out to be an old Indian saree, intricately embroidered with gold thread.

“This belonged to your great-great-grandmother,” said Grandmother softly, holding up the saree. “She brought it from India over a hundred years ago when she came as an indentured labourer.”

Anita listened in silence as her grandmother told the full story. She learned that their ancestor had left her family behind and sailed across the ocean on a crowded ship, hoping for a better life. She had worked hard on the sugar cane fields for many years before eventually owning a small plot of land.

“She was a very brave woman,” said Anita quietly.

“Yes,” said Grandmother. “And everything we have today is because of her courage.”

Anita carefully folded the saree and placed it back in the chest. She decided she would research her family’s history and write it all down in a notebook, so that her own children would one day know where they came from and feel proud of their heritage.

That evening, she began writing. The first line was: “My great-great-grandmother was a hero.”
