Question 4A — Reading Comprehension: Informational Text (10 marks)

Read the text below, then complete the sentences that follow.

Solar Energy in Mauritius

Solar energy is energy produced from sunlight. It is one of the cleanest and most plentiful sources of power available on Earth. Solar panels, also known as photovoltaic cells, are flat devices that capture sunlight and convert it into electricity. They are made from a special material called silicon, which absorbs light and releases electrons to create an electric current.

In Mauritius, the use of solar energy has grown rapidly in recent years. Many homes, schools and businesses have installed solar panels on their rooftops. The government has set a target to generate thirty-five percent of the country’s electricity from renewable sources by 2025. Solar farms — large areas covered with rows of solar panels — have also been built in several parts of the island.

Solar energy has many advantages. It is free and renewable, meaning it will never run out while the sun shines. It produces no harmful gases, unlike coal or oil. Once panels are installed, running costs are very low. In remote areas of Mauritius, solar panels provide electricity to communities that are not connected to the main power grid.

However, there are some challenges. Solar panels can be expensive to buy and install. They only produce electricity during daylight hours, and cloudy weather reduces their efficiency. Scientists are working to develop better battery systems to store solar energy for use at night or during bad weather. Solar energy represents an important part of Mauritius’s clean energy future.

Question 4B — Reading Comprehension: Narrative (15 marks)

Priya’s Invention

Priya was a twelve-year-old girl who had always been fascinated by science. When her teacher announced the annual school science fair, Priya immediately had an idea: she would build a small solar-powered fan.

She spent two weeks working on her project. She researched solar panels and bought a small kit from a local shop. Every evening after school, she sat at the kitchen table, carefully connecting wires and testing the circuit. Her younger brother Jai sat beside her, handing her tools and asking endless questions.

“Why does it need a battery?” Jai asked one evening.

“Because when there is no sun, the battery stores the energy so the fan can still work,” Priya explained patiently.

On the day of the science fair, Priya set up her model on the table. When the judges approached, she placed the solar panel under the classroom light and switched on the fan. It whirred into life immediately. The judges smiled and made careful notes on their clipboards.

“Can you explain how it works?” asked one judge.

Priya spoke clearly and confidently. She explained how light hit the solar panel, created electricity and powered the motor in the fan. The judges were visibly impressed.

At the award ceremony that afternoon, Priya was called to the stage to receive the first-prize trophy. The hall erupted in applause. Her parents hugged her tightly. Jai pulled her sleeve and whispered, “I knew you would win.” Priya laughed and hugged him back. It had been the best day of her life.
