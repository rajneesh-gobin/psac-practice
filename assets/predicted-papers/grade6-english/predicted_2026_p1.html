The Coral Reefs of Mauritius

The coral reefs of Mauritius are among the most remarkable natural treasures of the Indian Ocean. They form a ring of shallow, clear water around much of the island, creating sheltered lagoons that are perfect for swimming and snorkelling. The reefs are built by millions of tiny sea creatures called coral polyps, which build hard limestone structures over hundreds of years.

These reefs support an extraordinary variety of marine life. Hundreds of species of colourful fish dart through the coral branches, while sea turtles glide gracefully above the sandy seabed. Starfish, sea urchins, octopuses and moray eels all make their homes in the reef. Scientists have identified over one thousand species of plants and animals living in Mauritius’s lagoons.

Unfortunately, coral reefs around the world are under serious threat. Rising ocean temperatures caused by climate change lead to a process called bleaching, in which coral turns white and eventually dies. Pollution from boats, fertilisers and untreated waste also damages the reef. Careless tourists who stand on coral or remove pieces as souvenirs cause damage that can take decades to recover.

The Mauritian government has established marine parks and strict rules to protect the reefs. Fishing is restricted in certain zones, and boats must anchor away from the coral. Environmental groups run clean-up campaigns and educate the public about reef conservation. Every Mauritian has a role to play in keeping these extraordinary ecosystems safe for future generations.

Question 4B — Reading Comprehension: Narrative (15 marks)

The Butterfly

Meera was a lively ten-year-old girl who loved spending Saturday mornings in her grandmother’s garden. She enjoyed watering the plants, chasing butterflies and watching bees collect nectar from the flowers.

One bright morning, she spotted something unusual near the rose bushes. A beautiful butterfly with orange and black wings was lying very still on the ground. Meera crouched down carefully and saw that one of its delicate wings had become stuck to a dried leaf. The butterfly was struggling helplessly.

“Poor little thing,” Meera murmured softly.

She fetched a thin twig from the garden and, very gently, pried the wing free without touching it. For a long moment, nothing happened. Then, slowly, the butterfly spread its wings. It trembled once, twice — and in a burst of colour, it rose into the air and circled above Meera’s head before settling on a nearby flower.

“Grandma! Grandma!” Meera ran inside, calling excitedly. “I saved a butterfly!”

Her grandmother listened to the story and smiled warmly. “You have shown real kindness and patience,” she said. “Those are the finest qualities a person can have.”

That night, as she lay in bed, Meera made a decision. She would plant more flowers in the garden so that butterflies would always have a safe place to live. She felt a warm glow of happiness — she had made a small difference in the world, and that felt wonderful.
