Source A: Economic Transformation of Mauritius
"At independence in 1968, Mauritius depended almost entirely on sugar cane. Sugar made up 93% of export earnings and employed over 35% of the workforce. In the 1970s, the government established Export Processing Zones (EPZs) to attract foreign investment in manufacturing, particularly textiles. In the 1980s, tourism was developed as a third pillar. Since the 1990s, Mauritius has developed into a financial and ICT services hub. Today, no single sector dominates and Mauritius is described as a diversified, middle-income economy."

Source B: Post-independence development
"At independence in 1968, Mauritius was considered a poor country with few prospects. Experts warned of overpopulation and poverty. Yet within three decades, Mauritius had transformed itself into one of Africa's most prosperous nations. Universal free primary and secondary education was introduced, improving literacy rates dramatically. A public health system was established, reducing infant mortality. Investment in new industries created jobs for thousands. By the 1990s, Mauritius was regarded as an economic miracle and a model of successful development in the developing world."
