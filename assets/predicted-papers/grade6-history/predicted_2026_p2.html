Source A: Conditions of enslaved people in Mauritius
"Enslaved people in Mauritius had no legal rights. They could be bought and sold, beaten and punished at the will of their owners. They were forced to work on sugar plantations from sunrise to sunset with little rest. Their children were also enslaved. Despite these terrible conditions, enslaved people found ways to preserve their culture, including through music, dance and religion. Some escaped into the mountains, where they formed communities of Maroons — free people who refused to submit to slavery. The most famous place associated with Maroon resistance is Le Morne Brabant."

Source B: The indentured labour system
"Indentured labourers signed a contract (an indenture) agreeing to work in Mauritius for a fixed period, usually five years. In return, their passage to Mauritius was paid and they received food, housing and a small wage. When they arrived, they were assigned to a plantation and had to fulfil their contract before they could move freely. Many workers found conditions very harsh and had little freedom during their contract period. The landing site for all incoming labourers was Aapravasi Ghat in Port Louis, which is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site."
(i)
