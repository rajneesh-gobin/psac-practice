Source A: Le Morne Cultural Landscape
"Le Morne Brabant is a rugged mountain on the south-west peninsula of Mauritius. During the period of slavery (18th and early 19th century), it was used as a refuge by Maroon communities — enslaved people who had escaped their captivity and sought freedom in its caves and forests. The landscape is a symbol of the resistance against slavery and of the suffering of enslaved people. When UNESCO inscribed it as a World Heritage Site in 2008, it recognised Le Morne as an outstanding symbol of human courage in the struggle for freedom. A tragic legend tells of Maroons who jumped from the cliff in 1835, not knowing that slavery had just been abolished and that the soldiers climbing the mountain had come to bring them the news of freedom."

Source B: Living standards in Mauritius
"Mauritius ranks among the highest in Africa on the United Nations Human Development Index (HDI), which measures life expectancy, education and income. Access to clean water and sanitation is nearly universal. Healthcare is free to all citizens. Literacy rates are high, with over 90% of adults able to read and write. However, inequality remains a challenge — the gap between the richest and poorest households is significant. The government has introduced social welfare programmes to help the most vulnerable citizens."
