Read Source B carefully and answer the questions below. [6]
Source B
"Under French rule, sugar cane cultivation spread rapidly across Mauritius. The French brought enslaved people from Africa and Madagascar to work on the sugar plantations. By 1810, there were over 60,000 enslaved people on the island, compared to fewer than 7,000 free settlers. The enslaved people had no rights and lived and worked in very harsh conditions. They were bought and sold like property. Despite this, many enslaved people resisted their captors, and some escaped into the forests of the Black River area."

Source C: Population and Settlement in Mauritius

Mauritius has a population of about 1.3 million people. Most people live in towns and villages on the coastal lowlands and the lower slopes of the Central Plateau. Port Louis, the capital, is the largest city with about 150,000 inhabitants. Other important towns include Curepipe, Vacoas-Phoenix, and Quatre Bornes. The island is densely populated, with most agricultural activity taking place in the sugar-growing areas of the north and east.
