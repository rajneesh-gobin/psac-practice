1 (a) Any two valid chains: e.g. Grass → Rabbit → Hawk; Grass → Grasshopper → Hawk. [1 each]2
(b) A food web shows all the feeding relationships between organisms in an ecosystem. A food chain shows only one pathway; a web shows multiple interconnected chains. [1+1]2
(c) Grasshopper: would increase (no snakes to eat them) [1+reason 1]. Hawk: likely to increase initially (more grasshoppers to eat) [1+reason 1].4
(d)Decomposers (bacteria/fungi/microorganisms)1
(e)The flow of energy (from one organism to the next)1
2 (a) Habitat = the natural environment where an organism lives and finds food, shelter, water. Example: coral reef / mangrove forest / sugarcane field / forest. [1+1]2
(b) Camel: hump (stores fat for energy/water), large feet (spread weight on sand), long lashes (protect eyes from sand). Polar bear: thick white fur (insulation + camouflage), layer of blubber (warmth). Cactus: thick waxy stem (stores water), spines instead of leaves (reduce water loss). [1 adaptation + 1 reason per organism, max 6]6
(c) Structural = a physical feature of the body (e.g. thick fur). Behavioural = something the animal does (e.g. hibernation, migration). [1 each + 1 example each, max 2]2
3 (a) Any three: deforestation, pollution, overfishing, hunting/poaching, urbanisation, introduction of invasive species, climate change. [1 each]3
(b) Any two: hunted by humans (and introduced animals like pigs/rats); habitat destruction; could not fly to escape predators; laid only one egg at a time. [1 each]2
(c) Biodiversity = the variety of living species in an ecosystem [1]. Important because: greater variety means the ecosystem is more stable and resilient; if one species is lost, others can fill its role [1].2
(d) Any two: create nature reserves/national parks; breeding programmes in captivity; ban hunting/poaching; protect habitats from development. [1 each]2
(e) Carnivore = an animal that eats only other animals. Example from web: Fox, Hawk, Snake. [1]1
4 (a) Window pane: glass; transparent (allows light through). Electrical wire insulation: plastic/rubber; electrical insulator (does not conduct electricity). Bridge: steel/concrete; strong, rigid, high tensile strength. [2 per row correct: material + property]6
(b) Conductor: allows heat to flow through easily (e.g. metal/copper). Insulator: does not allow heat to flow easily (e.g. wood, plastic, wool). [1 per correct definition + 1 per example, max 3]3
5 (a) Ice melting: reversible (water can be frozen again). Wood burning: irreversible (ash cannot become wood again). Cake baking: irreversible (cannot un-bake a cake). [2 per row]6
(b) Irreversible. Substances needed: water and oxygen (air). [1+2]2
6 (a) Any three: rock/stone (building), iron/metal (tools), clay (pottery/bricks), sand (glass), coal (fuel), oil (plastic/fuel), limestone (cement). [1 per correct pair]3
(b) Natural material: found in nature, not made by humans (e.g. wood, cotton). Synthetic: made by humans/in a factory (e.g. plastic, nylon). [1 per correct definition + example]2
(c) Problem: plastic stays in the environment for hundreds of years; harms animals that eat it or get tangled; pollutes oceans and land [1]. Reduction: use reusable bags/bottles; recycle plastic; ban single-use plastics [1]. [1 per correct idea, max 2 + 1 for suggestion]3
7 (a) Two arrows on book: downward arrow labelled "Weight/Gravity" and upward arrow labelled "Normal reaction/Support force". [1 each]2
(b) Pulls towards Earth → Gravity. Opposes motion → Friction. Pulls magnetic materials → Magnetic attraction. Pushes apart → Magnetic repulsion. [1 each]4
(c) Friction between the book and table opposes the motion, slowing the book down [1]. In this case friction is harmful (it stops the book moving) [1].2
(d)Gravity (the gravitational pull of the Earth)1
8 (a) See-saw: lever. Ramp: inclined plane. Flagpole: pulley. Wheel on hub: wheel and axle. [1 each]4
(b) A = Load, B = Fulcrum, C = Effort. [1 each]3
(c) Moment = force × distance from fulcrum. A longer effort arm increases the moment produced by a smaller effort force, so less effort is needed to balance (lift) the same load. [1+1]2
9 (a) Pressure = the force acting per unit area. Formula: Pressure = Force ÷ Area (P = F/A). [1+1]2
(b) P = 60 N ÷ 0.03 m² = 2000 Pa (N/m²). [1 for correct substitution, 1 for correct calculation, 1 for unit]3
(c) The hollow shape of the ship displaces a large volume of water; the upthrust (upward force from the water) equals the weight of water displaced. If this upthrust equals the ship's weight, the ship floats. [1+1]2
10 (a) Igneous: forms when molten rock (magma/lava) cools and solidifies; example: granite, basalt. Sedimentary: forms from layers of sediment compacted over time; example: sandstone, limestone. Metamorphic: forms when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure; example: marble, slate. [2 per row]6
(b) Rock cycle = the continuous process by which rocks are formed, broken down and reformed [1]. Igneous rock is weathered by wind/rain into small particles → transported and deposited in layers → compressed over time to form sedimentary rock [1 per step, max 2].3
(c) Weathering = the breakdown of rocks into smaller pieces. Agents: water/rain/frost action, wind, temperature changes, acid rain, tree roots. [1 for definition, 2 agents = 1 mark]1
11 (a) Any four: rock particles/minerals, humus (decayed organic matter), water, air/gases, living organisms. [1 each]4
(b)(i) Clay; clay particles are very small and packed tightly together, so there are tiny air spaces and water cannot drain quickly. [1+1]2
(b)(ii) Loam; it drains well enough not to waterlog but retains enough moisture and nutrients for plant growth. [1+1]2
(c) Soil erosion = when topsoil is worn away by wind or water [1]. Prevention: plant trees/grass to hold soil; terracing on slopes; use windbreaks [1].2
