Three different areas — and who gets which

The app is not one screen with more buttons for grown-ups. A parent, a teacher and a child sign in differently and see genuinely different things. This is the part worth reading first.

👨‍👩‍👧Parent area

Signs in with an email address and password.

  • Owns the account and the family — up to 3 children.
  • Sees everything each child does, chapter by chapter.
  • Sets homework, locks chapters, caps difficulty.
  • Holds the billing, when paid plans open.
  • Creates each child's username and PIN, and resets a forgotten one.

This is the account you create when you sign up.

🧒Child area

Signs in with a family name, a username and a 4-digit PIN. No email address at all.

  • Practises, takes mocks, reads the syllabus, earns badges.
  • Sees only their own work — not a sibling's, not anyone else's.
  • Cannot open the parent area, and cannot lift their own limits.
  • Signed in on one device at a time.
  • Can pick their own colours, text size and sounds.

Created by the parent. Nothing to set up on the child's side.

👩‍🏫Teacher area

Signs in with an email address — then an administrator has to approve the account.

  • Builds a homework set from any subject and topics.
  • Shares one link with a class. Pupils need no account.
  • Sees results for the work they set — nothing else about a pupil.
  • Can grant a pupil a retry.
  • Not self-serve: the role is granted, never switched on by the user.

A teacher can also be a parent, with their own children on the same login.

📚 Chapter practice

Work through one topic at a time

Pick a subject, then a chapter. Questions are marked the instant they are answered — no waiting, no submitting a whole page and hoping.

  • Four difficulty levels — Basic, Medium, Hard, and Challenge (real word problems, not just harder sums).
  • Three-step hints — a nudge first, then a clue, then the method. The child chooses how much help to take.
  • A worked explanation on every question, so a wrong answer teaches something.
  • Diagrams and photos — clocks to read, bar charts, maps of Mauritius, angle diagrams. Tap any picture to enlarge it.
  • Pause and come back — a half-finished round is still there tomorrow.

📝 Mock exams

A real paper, under real time pressure

Practice tells you what a child knows. An exam tells you whether they can do it in 40 minutes with a clock running.

  • Full paper or a quick 10 — a complete 40-question mock, or a short one before bed.
  • Flag and come back, just like the real thing, with a question grid to jump around.
  • A printable version — same paper, exam layout, no timer. Print it or save as PDF and do it on paper.
  • Full review afterwards — every question, what they answered, what was right, and why. Filter to just the ones they got wrong.
  • The screen stays awake and locks to portrait for the duration.

📋 Tab 2 · Homework

Set work, and decide how much help they get

Assign a specific chapter instead of leaving them to choose. It appears on their home screen as a task to finish.

  • Pick the subject and the chapter — or leave the chapter open and assign the whole subject.
  • Choose the difficulty, or leave it mixed so they get a spread.
  • Add a note — “do this before Friday”, “the ones we did on Sunday”. They see it with the task.
  • Show answers: on or off. Off turns it into a test — they still get a score, but not the answers to copy.
  • Allow hints: on or off. Off means no three-step help on this particular piece of work.
  • Assign as many as you like, and delete one that is no longer relevant.

Homework does not count against a daily question limit — work you set must always be finishable.

🎛️ Tab 3 · Controls

Keep them on the part of the course they are on

A Grade 5 child does not need to stumble into Grade 6 work in week two. These controls apply to that child only.

  • Lock individual chapters. Locked ones show a padlock and cannot be opened — including inside exams and printable papers.
  • Cap the difficulty. Stop Challenge word problems appearing until they are ready for them.
  • Switch mock exams off entirely if timed papers are causing more stress than they are worth.
  • Turn off the tips — the little coaching callouts — for a child who finds them distracting.
  • Set defaults for all children at once from Account & Settings, instead of repeating yourself per child.

Changing a control takes effect on their next screen — you do not have to sign them out for it to apply.

Admin role management

What each role can do
Can… 👨‍👩‍👧 Parent 👩‍🏫 Teacher 🛡️ Admin 👑 Super
Manage own children✅✅✅✅
Set homework for a class—✅—✅
Open the admin panel——✅✅
Edit plans, questions, settings——✅✅
Roles / Plans / Create tabs———✅

These three roles are fixed in the database, not configurable here. profiles.role carries a CHECK constraint allowing only parent, teacher and admin, and is_admin() — literally role = 'admin' — gates 60 row-level-security policies. A fourth role would be rejected by the constraint, and would grant nothing even if it were not, because no policy would recognise it.

👑 Super admin is a separate flag (profiles.is_super_admin), not a role. It is set in SQL only, deliberately: it is what protects an admin from being demoted by another admin. A teacher additionally needs approved status in the Teachers tab — the role alone grants nothing.
