# NCE Grade 9 — English (N500): empirical exam blueprint

Derived by reading five real MES papers page by page. **Every page of every PDF
was rendered to PNG at 110 dpi and looked at.** Nothing below is inferred from
text extraction alone; `pdftotext` was used only to count words in passages,
and where a stimulus is a raster image that is stated explicitly.

| PDF | pages (`pdfinfo`) | pages visually inspected |
|---|---|---|
| `2021-English.pdf` | 20 | 20 / 20 |
| `2022-English 2021 2022.pdf` | 24 | 24 / 24 |
| `2023-English.pdf` | 24 | 24 / 24 |
| `2024-English.pdf` | 24 | 24 / 24 |
| `NCE-2025-English (1).pdf` | 24 | 24 / 24 |
| **total** | **116** | **116 / 116** |

No page was unreadable. Blank/`BLANK PAGE` fillers are included in the counts
(2021 pp. 19–20; 2022 pp. 23–24; 2023 pp. 23–24; 2024 pp. 23–24; 2025 pp. 23–24).

## 1. Per-year structure

| | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper code | N500 | N500 | N500 | N500 | N500 |
| Duration | 2 h 15 min | 2 h 15 min | 2 h 15 min | 2 h 15 min | 2 h 15 min |
| Total marks | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Questions | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Mark-bearing parts | 69 | 69 | 69 | 70 | 71 |
| Printed pages of paper | 17 | 21 (numbered 2–22) | 21 | 21 | 21 |
| **Named sections?** | **No** | **No** | **No** | **No** | **No** |
| Optional choice | none | Q10 A *or* B | Q10 A *or* B | Q10 A *or* B | Q10 A *or* B |
| Answer booklet | answers written on the question paper | same | same | same | same |

**There are no named sections in any year.** The paper is a flat run of
Question 1 → Question 10 with no "Reading / Writing / Language" headings and no
part/section letters. The only structural label that exists anywhere is
`Option A – Poetry` / `Option B – Prose` inside Q10 (2022 onwards). The skill
grouping is implicit but perfectly stable, and is what the marks-by-skill table
below reconstructs.

The question-by-question mark allocation is **identical in all five years**:

| Q | marks | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 15 | language work — 15 one-mark items |
| Q2 | 5 | vocabulary MCQ — 5 one-mark items |
| Q3 | 10 | non-fiction/visual text, 10 one-mark retrieval items |
| Q4 | 5 | word-formation cloze, 5 gaps |
| Q5 | 5 | error correction, 5 underlined errors |
| Q6 | 5 | open cloze, 5 gaps |
| Q7 | 10 | guided short writing, 50–75 words |
| Q8 | 20 | comprehension of a narrative passage |
| Q9 | 15 | essay, 200–250 words, choice of 3 |
| Q10 | 10 | literature appreciation (poetry; prose alternative from 2022) |

Five years, zero variation in the top-level allocation. This is the single most
important fact in this document.

|---|---|---|
| 1-mark parts | 63 / 62 / 61 / 63 / 65 | 62.8 % |

after 2022: 2021 Q8(14) gave 5 words for [5], 2022 Q8(14) gave 4 words for [4].
From 2023 the same task is worth [3] (three words), and from 2024 it is MCQ.

1+1+1+1+1+1+1+3, both open with narrative voice and close with a 3-mark
supported judgement. A child can prepare one shape for both.

### Formats that never appear

Measured absent from all five papers: true/false with justification; summary
or précis writing; formal letter of complaint/application; email; dialogue or
play-script writing; matching/table-completion (beyond 2021 Q3's information
sheet); gap-fill from a supplied word bank; any listening or speaking component.
Also absent: any question requiring a set text read in advance — every literature
extract is printed in full with a glossary.

## 6. Numbering depth and answer-line conventions

**Depth actually used — never more than four levels, and level 4 is only MCQ options:**

```
Question 8 (20 marks)          ← level 1: the question, marks in the heading
  From Paragraph 3             ← navigational signpost, not numbered
  9.  ...                      ← level 2: item number
      (i) / (ii)               ← level 3: parts of a 2-mark answer
  15. ...
      (i) crept (line 20):     ← level 3
          A  moved angrily     ← level 4: MCQ options, always A–D
```

- Q1/Q2/Q3/Q8 number their items `1.`…`n.` (2021 and 2022 use `1)` in Q8; 2023
  onwards use `1.`).
- Sub-parts are `(i)/(ii)/(iii)` **or** `(a)/(b)/(c)` — both conventions appear,
  sometimes in the same paper (2024 Q10A uses `(a)/(b)`, 2025 Q10A uses `(i)/(ii)`).
- MCQ options are **always** `A B C D`, one per line, four options, no "none of
  the above". The narrative-voice item always offers the impossible
  `D fourth-person` as its distractor — in all five years, in both Q10 options.
- Marks appear in the **right margin in square brackets** `[1]` `[2]` `[3]` on
  every mark-bearing part of Q8 and Q10, and in the question heading for the
  whole-question value. Q1/Q2/Q4/Q5/Q6 usually give the value once in the rubric
  ("Each item carries one mark") rather than per item; 2024 Q2 is the exception
  and marks every item `[1]`.
- A running `Marks` column is ruled down the right of every page, with an
  examiner/team-leader/CE grid at the foot of the last page of each question.

**Ruled answer lines per mark** — counted from the rendered pages:

| task | marks | ruled lines given |
|---|---|---|
| short comprehension answer | 1 | 1–2 dotted lines |
| word hunt / "pick out the word" | 1 | 1 line |
| "give two reasons" | 2 | 2 lines, pre-labelled `(i)` `(ii)` |
| "explain fully" | 2 | 2 lines |
| vocabulary gloss (open) | 1 per word | 1 line per word |
| "what is this poem about?" | 3 | 7–8 lines |
| guided short writing, 50–75 words | 10 | 10–11 lines plus printed salutation and sign-off |
| essay, 200–250 words | 15 | ~32 lines over two full pages (≈12–13 on the first page below the prompt, ≈20 on the second) |

That is roughly **one ruled line per 7–8 words of the target length** for the two
extended tasks, and **one to two lines per mark** everywhere else. A child who
writes past the lines is writing past what the mark scheme wants.

## 7. What the papers test that a syllabus list does not say

Reading the five papers against the chapter list in
`subjects/grade9-english/_manifest.js`, several things are load-bearing in the
exam and invisible in a syllabus statement:

1. **Nothing is examined by name.** No question ever says "adverb", "determiner"
   or "quantifier". Q1 asks for a rewrite or a circled option; the grammar is the
   *means*, never the *object*. A chapter that teaches terminology teaches the
   wrong thing for this paper.
2. **The paper is a fixed instrument, not a topic sample.** Ten questions, the
   same ten mark values, five years running. Preparation is best organised by
   *question number* — "how to do Q5", "how to do Q10" — rather than by topic.
3. **Reading is measured on narrative fiction only.** The syllabus says "complex
   and literary texts" and "compare viewpoints across texts"; the paper has never
   once set two texts to compare, never set non-fiction prose for comprehension,
   and never set anything but a story for the 20-mark question.
4. **Retrieval from non-continuous text is worth 10 % on its own** (Q3: posters,
   adverts, fact boxes). That is a real skill — scanning a layout — and no chapter
   in the manifest mentions it.
5. **Proofreading somebody else's writing is worth 5 % on its own** (Q5), and it
   is a different act from writing correctly yourself. The syllabus folds it into
   "proofread for grammar, spelling and punctuation" under Writing; the paper
   makes it a standalone question with a worked example.
6. **Morphology is worth 5 % on its own** (Q4: `mystery → mysterious`,
   `real → really`, `adopt → adopted`). No chapter covers word formation.
7. **A small fixed vocabulary of literary terms is compulsory**: simile, metaphor,
   personification, rhyme, narrative voice, and "what is the effect of…". Five or
   six of the ten Q10 marks turn on these in every paper, in both options.
8. **Listening and Speaking are never examined.** Two chapters (`g9eng-listening`,
   `g9eng-speaking`, 6 of the manifest's 34 weight units) address skills the N500
   written paper cannot reach.
9. **Registers that never appear**: formal letters, emails, reports, summaries.
   The 10-mark writing task is always informal and always bulleted for the child.
10. **Fourth-person is always an option and never an answer.** Trivial, but it
    means the narrative-voice item is really a three-way choice, and a child who
    knows that starts one distractor ahead.

  2023 Q1(3)(5), 2024 Q1(3)(7), 2025 Q1(1), 2023 Q5 *on→in*, 2025 Q5 *of→in*),
  adjectives 1.6 (comparatives/superlatives 2022 Q1(5), 2023 Q1(1), 2024 Q1(1),
  2025 Q1(4)), adverbs 1.0 (2025 Q1(3), plus adverb formation in Q4 2024 *real*),
  nouns 1.0 (plurals 2021 Q1(10), 2025 Q1(11), noun formation in Q4).
- **Modals 0.2**: measured once in five papers (2024 Q1(9) *will*, and even that
  reads as future tense rather than modality). It is at the floor, not zero, only
  because `Math.max(1, …)` cannot express zero anyway.

### 8.2 Paper areas with **no chapter at all** — 16 marks a year unhoused

These are real, recurring, mark-bearing tasks that no existing chapter covers.
**They are the most important finding in this section.**

| unhoused area | marks/yr | evidence | proposed new chapter |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vocabulary & word choice** (Q2 in full, plus the in-context vocabulary item at the end of Q8) | **8** (5 + 3; was 5+5 in 2021) | 2024 Q2(5) *musicians*; 2025 Q8(15) *queried* | `g9eng-vocabulary` — MCQ collocation and vocabulary-in-context, weight **3** |

Q4, Q5 and Q6 overlap other chapters (a Q4 gap is often a verb form; a Q5 error
is often a pronoun), which is why 6 of those marks are already counted inside

### 8.3 Chapters the papers never test

- **`g9eng-listening` (weight 3)** — zero marks in five papers. The N500 is a
  written paper with no audio component. Its syllabus text ("listen critically to
  extended spoken and audio-visual texts") describes a classroom activity, not an
  examined skill.
- **`g9eng-speaking` (weight 3)** — zero marks in five papers, same reason.

Together they hold **6 of the manifest's 34 weight units — 17.6 % of the exam
draw — for 0 % of the exam.** If the pack is ever switched on with these weights,
roughly one question in six of a Grade 9 English mock exam would test something
the real paper cannot ask. **Proposal: set both to `examWeight: 0`, keep the
chapters** (they are real syllabus content and belong on the Syllabus screen; a
0-weight chapter still shows and can still be practised) — but note the trap
recorded in `CLAUDE.md`: `assembleExamPaper()` clamps with `Math.max(1, …)`, so a
0 weight does **not** by itself keep a chapter out of an exam. The reliable
exclusion for a chapter with no poolable questions is the type filter, and while
these two chapters have no questions at all they cannot be dealt anyway.

### 8.5 Caveats on this proposal

- **Q7 and Q9 are not gradable by this app.** 25 % of the real paper is free
  extended writing with no correct answer. `g9eng-writing` at weight 10 would
  deal 10 questions of a 40-question exam that the engine can only mark if they
  are recast as something objective (best opening sentence, which bullet is
  unanswered, ordering a paragraph). That recasting is a content decision, not a
  weighting one, and it should be made before this weight is applied — otherwise
  the largest single chapter in the pack is the one that cannot score.
- **Q10's two options are alternatives**, so a child sits 10 marks of literature,
  not 20. The weight above reflects 10.
- The grammar residue in 8.1 is the least certain number here. Assigning a
  four-option gap-fill to one word class is a reading, and a different reader
  would shift a mark or two between pronouns, determiners and prepositions. The
  ordering is stable; the second decimal place is not. All of those chapters land
  at the weight floor of 1 regardless.
- **Five papers is the whole sample.** The structural finding (35/30/25/10) is as
  strong as five identical years can make it. The within-Q1 finding (which word
  classes come up) is a sample of 75 items and should be re-measured if more
  papers become available.
