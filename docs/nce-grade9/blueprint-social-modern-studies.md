# NCE Grade 9 — Social & Modern Studies (N570): empirical exam blueprint

Derived by reading five real MES papers page by page. **Every page of every PDF
was rendered to PNG at 110 dpi and looked at with an image reader.** These
papers are built on maps, photographs, logos, tables, pyramids and cyclone
tracks, and `pdftotext` destroys every one of them — extraction was used only to
transcribe mark allocations and question wording, never to decide what a
question contained.

| PDF | pages (`pdfinfo`) | pages visually inspected |
|---|---|---|
| `Social-and-Modern-Studies.pdf` (March/April **2021**) | 16 | 16 / 16 |
| `NCE-Social-and-Modern-Studies-2021-2022.pdf` (**2021-2022**) | 20 | 20 / 20 |
| `NCE-2023-Social-Modern-Studies.pdf` | 20 | 20 / 20 |
| `NCE-2024-Social-Modern-Studies.pdf` | 16 | 16 / 16 |
| `NCE-2025-Social-Modern-Studies.pdf` | 20 | 20 / 20 |
| **total** | **92** | **92 / 92** |

No page was unreadable and **no paper is a duplicate of another** — the two
2021-dated files are two different sittings (a March/April 2021 paper and a
"2021-2022" paper) with entirely different questions. Blank fillers are inside
the counts: 2021-2022 p. 2 (unlabelled blank) and pp. 18–20; 2023 pp. 19–20;
2025 p. 20.

Throughout this document a paper is cited by year and question, e.g.
**2025 Q4(a)(i)**. The 2021-2022 sitting is cited as **2122**.

The only positional constant is **Q1**, which is the objective opener in every
year: PART A a multiple-choice block, PART B a second objective block. Q1 has

### Section A is objective; Section B is not

Measured, mark by mark: the share of each section that is machine-markable
(MCQ, true/false, tick, match, order, gap-fill from a printed list or from
words in brackets) as against free written response.

| | 2021 | 2122 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section A objective marks | 35 / 50 | 46 / 50 | 50 / 50 | 36 / 50 | 48 / 50 | **215 / 250 = 86 %** |
| Section B objective marks | 0 / 50 | 5 / 50 | 3 / 50 | 0 / 50 | 4 / 50 | **12 / 250 = 4.8 %** |

Section A 2023 is 100 % objective. Section B is essentially never objective —
across 250 marks in five years only twelve are, and every one of those is a
one-word naming line inside a written question (2122 Q6(a), 2122 Q7(a)(i),
2023 Q7(a)(i)/(ii)/(iv), 2025 Q7(a) "Force:", 2025 Q7(b)(ii)).

Counted over **183 mark-bearing parts / 500 marks**. A "part" is one bracketed

Grouped: **1 mark 49 parts (26.8 %) · 2 marks 66 (36.1 %) · 3 marks 12 (6.6 %)
· 4 marks 32 (17.5 %) · 5+ marks 24 (13.1 %)**.

Three readings that matter:

- **The paper is built out of 1s and 2s.** 115 of 183 parts (62.8 %) are worth
  1 or 2 marks. Nothing above 4 marks exists outside the Q1 objective blocks
  and a handful of "give three, 2 marks each" bundles.
- **There is no essay anywhere in five years.** The largest free-response
  allocation is 6 marks (2021 Q7(b), 2023 Q9(c)) and both are printed as three
  numbered points at 2 marks each, on three separate answer lines. The paper
  never asks for continuous prose.
- **4 marks almost always means "two points × 2 marks", never one long answer.**
  Every 4-mark part in 2024 and 2025 is printed as two numbered stems with two
  answer lines each: 2024 Q9(b), 2024 Q10(c)(ii), 2025 Q9(a), 2025 Q10(b).
  The exception is 2024 Q7(d)(ii) and 2025 Q6(c)(ii), which are 2 × (explain,
  2 marks) against a named stimulus.

The 3-mark part is dying: 7 in 2021, 1 in 2122, 2 in 2023, 2 in 2024, **0 in
2025**. The 4-mark two-point bundle has replaced it.

its maps are labelled **Map 1** and **Map 3**. There is no Map 2 anywhere in
that paper. It is an editing artefact, not a missing page — every printed page
2–16 is present and was inspected.

## 5. The strand split — history / geography / civics

This is the measurement the pack most needs, because "Social & Modern Studies"
is one exam subject built out of three teachable strands, and a pack that blurs
them cannot be revised strand by strand.

**Every one of the 500 marks was assigned to exactly one strand**, part by part,
using these rules — stated explicitly because several parts sit on a boundary
and a different rule would move a few marks:

- **HISTORY** — anything anchored to a past period: colonial naming, indentured
  labour, the 1967 election and its parties, independence and the republic,
  living conditions in the 1940s–60s, the Chagos excision and expulsion, the
  sugar economy of the 1960s, import substitution, the EPZ, the "Mauritian
  Miracle", industrialisation as a process and its impacts.
- **GEOGRAPHY** — population studies (density, distribution, structure, birth
  and death rates, life expectancy, ageing), migration as movement, map / table
  / graph skill, the outer islands as places, natural hazards and environment.
- **CIVICS / MODERN STUDIES** — present-tense society and state: government,
  voting, the constitution, taxation and the welfare state, law and deviance,
  media, family and gender roles, forces driving social change, membership of
  international organisations and present-day bilateral links, economic sectors
  and factors of production.

Three boundary calls worth naming, because they are the ones that move marks:

- **Economic content is split by tense.** A period-anchored economic question is
  history (2024 Q7, the EPZ); a present-tense one is civics (2025 Q3(b), what a
  factory needs).
- **"Where our people came from" is civics, not history** (2122 Q5(a),
  2023 Q1(e)), because the syllabus itself files origins under links with the
  world.
- **Pure stimulus-reading is geography**, wherever its content sits. That moves
  four marks out of the family/social block: 2023 Q5(c) is a table of women's
  employment, but every one of its four marks is "read this value off the table".

| strand | 2021 | 2122 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | total marks | % of 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **History** | 38 | 28 | 29 | 38 | 27 | **160** | **32.0 %** |
| **Geography** | 20 | 22 | 37 | 22 | 27 | **128** | **25.6 %** |
| **Civics / modern studies** | 42 | 50 | 34 | 40 | 46 | **212** | **42.4 %** |
| total | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100 | 500 | |

- **Civics is the largest strand in every single year**, by between 2 and 22
  marks. This is the finding most likely to surprise anyone who reads the
  subject name as "history and geography with a bit extra".
- **History is second in three years of five**, and it swings hardest in
  absolute terms: 38 in 2021 and 2024 down to 27 in 2025.
- **Geography swings hardest in relative terms**: 20 marks in 2021, 37 in 2023 —
  an 85 % difference, driven almost entirely by whether cyclones appear
  (2023 Q6 alone is 8 geography marks plus 2 of map skill; there is no
  equivalent question in 2021, 2122 or 2024).
- **One paper is not a sample.** Cyclones: 10 marks in 2023, 1 in 2021, 1 in
  2025, **0** in 2122 and 2024. Migration: 9 marks in 2024, **0** in 2021.
  A pack weighted from a single paper would get both badly wrong. Three years
  is the floor; five is what this document used.

## 6. Numbering depth and answer-line conventions

Measured on the rendered pages, not inferred from extraction.

- **Depth is four levels in every year**, but the ladder differs:
  - 2021: `QUESTION 10` → `1.` → `(a)` → `(i)`
  - 2122: `QUESTION 10` → `1.` → `a)` → `(i)`
  - 2023 / 2024 / 2025: `Question 10` → `(a)` → `(i)` → `1.`
  The 2023-onward ladder (letter, roman, arabic) is settled across three years
  and is the one a new pack should mirror.
- **Q1 alone uses PART A / PART B** as a sub-heading. No other question does,
  in any year.
- **Mark brackets contradict the cover page in four of five papers.** Every
  cover says marks are "given in brackets ( )". 2021 does use round brackets —
  `(2 marks)`, `(8 Marks)`, inconsistently capitalised. **2122, 2023, 2024 and
  2025 all use square brackets** — `[4]`, with no word "marks" at all.
- **Answer lines are dotted leaders and their count tracks the marks**: 1 mark
  ≈ one short line, 2 marks ≈ two full lines, 4 marks ≈ two numbered stems of
  two lines each. A candidate can read the expected answer length off the page,
  and that is part of how the paper communicates its demand.
- Objective answers are written on a short dotted tail at the right of the
  statement (true/false), inside a drawn box (tick questions, 2023 Q4), or as a
  drawn line between two printed dots (matching, 2024 Q4(a)).
- A ruled **"Marks"** column runs down the right edge of every printed page in
  all five years.
- Examiner initial boxes (`E` / `TL` / `QC` / `CE`) appear at the foot of the
  right margin from 2122 onward; 2021 has them only in the cover grid.
- `Please turn over` appears at the foot of odd-numbered printed pages.
- `END OF PAPER` is printed in 2023, 2024 and 2025; **2021 and 2122 have no
  end-of-paper marker.**
- The cover grid lists Question 1–10 plus Total, and names Examiner (called
  "Marker" in 2021), Team Leader, Quality Controller (2021 and 2122 only) and
  CE/ACE.

## 7. What the papers test that the syllabus chapter list does not

`subjects/grade9-social-modern-studies/_manifest.js` currently declares three
chapters: *Socio-Economic Development since 1968*, *Our Links with the World*,
*Population Studies*. Reading those three descriptions as generously as the
text allows, they cover roughly **252 of 500 marks (50.4 %)**. The other
**248 marks (49.6 %) have nowhere to live.**

Named in order of how much of the paper they are. The last two rows are marked
*partly* because a generous reading of *"the socio-economic conditions of
Mauritius at the time of independence"* can be stretched to cover them — they
are counted **inside** the 252 above, and listed here because neither is named:

The two most costly omissions are **the government/welfare-state block and the

- *"Distinguish between migration, circulation and displacement"* is examined as
  migration / immigrant / refugee / push factors (2024 Q10, 2025 Q10,
  2023 Q5(b)(ii)) — **the word "circulation" appears nowhere in any of the five
  papers.**

### What the proposal changes against today's manifest

- The three existing chapters survive in spirit but not in shape.
  `g9sms-development` (weight 3) is measurably **five** chapters covering
  131 marks; `g9sms-population` (weight 3) is measurably **two** covering
  68 marks, with a third — `g9sms-map-data-skills`, 23 marks — that the
  manifest never mentions in any form; `g9sms-links` (weight 3) is the closest
  match at 53 marks, and the only one of the three whose measured weight (4) is
  near its declared one.
- Today's weights are 3 / 3 / 3 = 9. The measured shape is 40 spread over 17.
- **A note that should carry into the manifest when it is written**: unlike the
  PSAC packs, these mark maps come from **five** papers, and the strand totals
  move by up to 15 marks a year. Recording which papers a weight came from —
  as `scripts/exam-mark-maps.json` already does for the PSAC packs — matters
  more here, not less.

### Writing order suggested by the measurements

1. `g9sms-industrialisation` (56 marks), `g9sms-links` (53),
   `g9sms-population` (50) — 159 marks, 31.8 % of the exam, in three chapters.
2. `g9sms-government-welfare` (43), `g9sms-family` (41), `g9sms-media` (35) —

3. Everything else.

### Format coverage the app cannot reach today

Section A is 86 % objective and almost all of it maps onto existing factories:
`makeMCQ` covers the MCQ blocks, the true/false blocks, the tick blocks and the
bracket-choice gap-fills; `makeMatch` covers the join-the-dots and answer-grid
matching; `makeCloze` covers the word-list gap-fill passages. What does **not**
map:

| paper format | marks / 500 | nearest existing factory | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shade a map region against a key | 3 | none | needs a click-a-region interaction |
| Complete a bar on a population pyramid | 3 | none | needs a numeric-to-graphic input |
| Order pictures chronologically into boxes | 4 | none | needs an ordering / sequencing type |
| Free written response, ~2 marks per point | 238 | none | this is Section B — 95 % of it |

The honest reading: **an MCQ-and-match pack can faithfully cover Section A
(250 marks, half the exam) and almost none of Section B.** Section B should be
authored as taught content with model answers rather than as gradable items —
the same shape as the 164 past-paper items that already ship read-only with
`markScheme` self-marking, and for the same reason.

One further constraint the measurements imply: **34 % of questions carry a
photograph and 18 % carry a map**, all of Mauritius and its dependencies. Any
pack that reproduces the paper's shape needs bundled artwork under
`assets/questions/` with recorded provenance, and there is currently none for
Grade 9. Authoring picture-dependent items before that artwork exists produces
the same broken item the French `fr-images` chapters had to work around.
